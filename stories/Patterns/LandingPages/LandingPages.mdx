# Landing page patterns

> **Note:** Tracked on [#1112](https://github.com/unisdr/undrr-mangrove/issues/1112). Review against the [component checklist](https://github.com/unisdr/undrr-mangrove/blob/main/docs/REVIEW-CHECKLIST.md).

UNDRR publishes several distinct landing page shapes, and they are usually
discussed one page at a time. These examples put three of them side by side so
the structural differences are visible: what carries the title, whether the page
needs contents links, and how content is grouped below the fold.

The copy is illustrative. The structures follow the compositions in the
[Gutenberg content guide](https://assets.undrr.org/docs/gutenberg-content-guide/CONTENT_GUIDE.md)
and the pages currently published on undrr.org.

## Topic or initiative

A subject with several routes into it. A split hero carries the title, an icon
row answers "what would you like to do?", a tinted band explains why the subject
matters, and cards below it carry recent material.

This is the shape most likely to grow into a content hub. When people begin
returning to it repeatedly and moving sideways between its sections, it has
outgrown a landing page — see [Content hub](?path=/docs/patterns-content-hub--docs).

<Canvas of={Stories.TopicLanding} />

## Report or publication

One long document with a stable structure. A coloured masthead carries the title
and lede, and the contents sidebar is doing real work here rather than
decorating: the page is long, its sections are named, and readers arrive wanting
a specific one.

<Canvas of={Stories.ReportLanding} />

## Collection index

A route to many items rather than a subject in its own right. Subsection
headings group covers into rows; each group links onward to its full collection.
There is no hero, because the page is a directory and the covers are the
content.

## Choosing between them

| If the page…                                                      | Use                                                   | Because                                                            |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Introduces a subject and points several ways into it              | Topic                                                 | The routes are the content; the hero sets the subject              |
| Is one long document people cite sections of                      | Report                                                | Contents links and a stable reading column matter more than routes |
| Exists to reach many items                                        | Collection index                                      | Grouped rows scan faster than prose, and no hero is needed         |
| Is returned to repeatedly, with lateral movement between sections | [Content hub](?path=/docs/patterns-content-hub--docs) | It needs persistent identity and section navigation                |

Use the locale toolbar for Arabic and the theme toolbar for the other brands.
Every example sits under the standard UNDRR page header and global navigation,
so the vertical budget below the chrome is honest.

## Composition

These pages compose shipped components only: `Hero`, `IconCard`, `VerticalCard`,
`BookCard`, `TableOfContents`, `TextCta` and the breadcrumb styling. Their
`mg-landing-example` layout styles load only with these stories, and the parent
chrome comes from the shared `UndrrChrome` helper the content hub examples also
use. There are no production exports, hydration APIs or distributed CSS classes.

> **Implementation note (for contributors):** the tinted band and the report
> masthead both rely on `mg-container-full-width`, which bleeds an inherited
> background to the viewport edges from inside `mg-container`. That is the same
> mechanism the Gutenberg `wp:columns` background wrapper produces, so these
> examples stay honest about what an editor can actually build. The alternating
> plain and tinted bands are the main thing that makes a real UNDRR page feel
> substantial, and they are easy to leave out of a prototype.
