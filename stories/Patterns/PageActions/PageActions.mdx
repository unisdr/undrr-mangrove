# Page actions

How [ShareButtons](?path=/docs/components-buttons-sharebuttons--docs) and [Cite this](?path=/docs/components-cite-this--docs) sit together at the end of an article, publication, terminology or HIP page. This is a composition of existing components and utility classes, not a new component. Each component still works alone, and the publishing site decides which ones a page needs.

<Canvas of={Stories.ShareAndCite} />

## Why two components

They meet different needs. Share this sends the page to other people: social networks, email, a QR code, or the page's shortlink. Cite this is for referencing the work in the reader's own writing: a formatted citation, the work's lasting URL, and RIS or BibTeX files for reference managers. Cite this does not repeat the social buttons. Its one share-like action, Copy link, copies the citation's URL rather than the shortlink, because a citation needs a locator that lasts.

## Layout

- Stack the two in one container with `mg-u-flex mg-u-flex-column mg-u-gap-200`, Share this first.
- Add `mg-cite-this--secondary` to Cite this. Its outlined trigger sits more evenly with the quiet ShareButtons icons than the default filled button.
- When a page has only one of them, use the component alone. The default filled Cite this trigger suits a page where citing is the main action, such as a publication.

```html
<div class="mg-u-flex mg-u-flex-column mg-u-gap-200">
  <section data-mg-share-buttons data-main-label="Share this"></section>
  <div
    class="mg-cite-this mg-cite-this--secondary"
    data-mg-cite-this
    data-mg-cite-this-title="Hazard Information Profiles: 2025 version"
    data-mg-cite-this-author="United Nations Office for Disaster Risk Reduction"
    data-mg-cite-this-year="2025"
    data-mg-cite-this-publisher="UNDRR"
    data-mg-cite-this-type="report"
  ></div>
</div>
```

For a citation that stays readable without JavaScript, render the full Cite this markup shown in its documentation.
