# Hub header

> **Note:** A Storybook exploration for [#1112](https://github.com/unisdr/undrr-mangrove/issues/1112), not a published component. Review against the [component checklist](https://github.com/unisdr/undrr-mangrove/blob/main/docs/REVIEW-CHECKLIST.md).

One organism that owns a content hub's identity and its section navigation, with
an optional expressive treatment for pages that want the weight of a hero. See it
in a whole page in [Content hub](?path=/docs/example-content-hub--docs).

**The required parts are identity and navigation. Hero-like presentation is a
variant of this component, not the other way round.** That direction is the whole
point. A page that should not lead with a photograph — a deep guidance page, say —
drops to `compact` and keeps its navigation. If the links lived inside a hero,
that page would have to choose between a decoration it does not want and the
navigation it needs.

## Expressive

The hub home, and any page that wants gravity at the top. The banner carries the
title and lede; media is optional.

## Expressive without media

Most UNDRR pages carry a hero even with no photograph to show, because the top of
the page is where readers look. The banner works without media, so a page can have
that weight without inventing an image for it.

<Canvas of={Stories.ExpressiveWithoutMedia} />

## Unified with the banner

`background` sets the surface for the whole header. On a colour, the bar bleeds
to the viewport edges and meets the banner below it with no seam, so the two read
as one block — while staying separate containers, which is what keeps the
navigation's contrast under control instead of at the mercy of a photograph.

The colour variants use `Hero`'s own tokens, so a header and a hero set to the
same variant match across all five themes without anything being hardcoded.

## Compact on a colour

The same surface without a banner, for pages that want the hub to carry a key

## Compact

Everything the hub still needs, and nothing it does not. Identical in height and
treatment to the identity row of the expressive variant, which is what lets a
reader keep their bearings moving between page types.

## Behaviour

Section links are ordinary page links. Pass `current: "page"` for the exact
current page and `current: "location"` for an ancestor whose descendant is open —
the distinction GOV.UK's service navigation draws, and the reason this is not a
tab set. Give `navLabel` the hub's own name rather than a generic "sections", so
a page carrying several navigation landmarks keeps them distinguishable.

`navRef` exposes the navigation element, so the consuming page can keep the
active section scrolled into view.

> **Implementation note (for contributors):** the navigation keeps its own solid
> surface above the banner instead of sitting on it. `mg-hero` sets
> `color: neutral-0` on the element and, until recently, forced every descendant
> anchor to white — links placed over editor-chosen media have contrast nobody
> can predict, and the active-state marker would disappear against a light
> photograph. Keeping the two surfaces separate is what makes the active state
> safe to draw at all.
>
> Every surface-dependent value is a custom property, so a background variant
> only retargets those and the rest follows: the edge fades, the name divider and
> both focus rings all read the surface rather than assuming white. On a coloured
> ground each link goes white, so the current item can no longer be marked by
> colour — weight and the leading rule carry it, and neither was
> colour-dependent to begin with.
>
> The banner reuses hero _tokens and layout_, not `.mg-hero` itself. Taking the
> class would inherit the full-bleed breakout, the forced text colour and the
> fixed aspect ratio — three things this component immediately overrides.
