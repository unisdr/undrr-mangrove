# Content patterns

Content archetypes organize patterns by reader need rather than CMS bundle. **Article** covers news and commentary; **Event** covers general events and programme sessions; **Reference entry** covers definitions and hazard profiles; **Opportunity** starts with a job profile. Organization includes general and coordination-platform profiles.

Keep three decisions separate: **archetype/profile** describes editorial meaning, **presentation** selects a full page, teaser or card, and **layout** defines geometry such as a horizontal listing. A profile is not another card variant. Existing page examples appear beneath their owning archetype: Article, Publication, Collection and Landing page. Hub home is a Landing page variant; persistent hub navigation also supports detail pages without redefining their content types. Existing story and docs URLs remain available through explicit stable IDs.

Family files own presentation specifications and profile projections. The shared teaser renderer reads those projections without importing CMS bindings. Canonical models remain provisional; a teaser's optional fields do not define a complete page model.

## Drupal integration

Drupal is a major consumer. Its explicit bundle-to-archetype/profile bindings stay in Mangrove at `stories/Integrations/Drupal/contentPatterns.js` and are published as `DRUPAL_CONTENT_PATTERNS` from the ContentTeaser entry. News maps to Article/news and blog to Article/commentary. Bindings may evolve without reorganizing the pattern library. They select profiles only; production field adapters and domain/link policy still need implementation and review.

National platform provisionally maps to Organization/coordination-platform, preserving its existing teaser selection. An activity-led portal may map to Landing page instead; the consumer's purpose should determine that choice.

## Presentation coverage

**Experimental** means an implemented teaser with a provisional contract. **Reference** links an existing page example whose family contract is still provisional. **Stub** means not designed or implemented: a reserved place with no callable component or import. Shared card primitives are available, but content-family card patterns remain stubs until their field selection and mapping are specified.

## Building a presentation

Start from the content family's meaning and the reader's task. Define required and optional information, omission rules and source mapping for that presentation. Compose shared components after those decisions. An existing horizontal teaser does not establish the layout, fields or actions of a full page; a generic vertical card does not establish a content-specific card pattern.

For example, Resource has an implemented experimental teaser and explicit full-page/card stubs. Its full-page information architecture has not been specified. Publication has a report-page reference and a teaser, with its compact-card content pattern still a stub. Keep source taxonomies, dates and identity distinct even when a particular presentation combines labels for display.

## AI and integration

The generated `content-patterns.json` catalogue records every family's model and presentation status separately from the usable component catalogue. Stubs carry no import or component entry. AI agents must preserve those status distinctions and must not fabricate a working page from a stub. Drupal bindings are included in the discovery catalogue; field adapters, domain policy and canonical schema decisions remain explicit production gates.
