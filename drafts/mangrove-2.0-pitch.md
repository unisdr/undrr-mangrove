# Mangrove 2.0: the pitch

**Audience:** internal UNDRR engineers and stakeholders who build/maintain Drupal sites on Mangrove.
**Purpose:** make the case that 2.0 is worth adopting, and explain why it looks different from what people expect Mangrove to ship.
**Status:** draft strategy document, not published documentation. See §7, "What this document is not."

**The one-sentence version:** Mangrove 2.0 isn't "the library got nicer" — it's the point where making UNDRR's web experiences more consistent, more reliable, and genuinely better stops being something every product team does alone, hoping it converges by accident, and becomes something the whole organization can actually do *together*, because there's finally a shared place solid enough to do it in.

A version number is not, by itself, exciting. What's exciting is what the version number is standing in for: for most of Mangrove's life, there wasn't really anywhere for teams to converge — the vocabulary wasn't agreed, the plumbing didn't hold, sub-brands couldn't adopt it without losing themselves in it. That's the actual reason cross-team consistency has had to be re-invented, team by team, site by site, for years. 2.0 doesn't fix that by decree. It fixes it by finally being a place worth showing up to. The ask this pitch is really making isn't "please use this library" — it's "the meeting point now exists; what's missing is participation."

**One shared experience, felt differently by everyone who touches it.** The same consistency argument reads differently depending on who's standing where: it means one thing to a developer (a predictable API instead of rebuilding the same card for the fifth time), another to a content editor or program office (a page that looks and behaves the same way no matter which team built it), and another again to the actual visitor to a UNDRR site (an experience that feels like one coherent organization, not a federation of independently-designed properties). Naming all three audiences explicitly, in one place, is itself part of the pitch — most people only ever see their own slice.

*Open item, not yet filled in: a concrete map of where Mangrove is actually experienced today — which Drupal sites, which of the static microsites, which other surfaces (the risk and resilience work was mentioned as one) — so this section can name real places instead of speaking abstractly. The person who owns this pitch is planning to supply that list; treat this paragraph as a placeholder until it's filled in, not as a claim about current reach.*

## 1. The adoption problem, named directly

There is a real, current perception among UNDRR engineers that Mangrove is "too heavy, too slow, too stale, too dated." It's not a baseless perception — it's a reasonable read of what 1.x looked like from the outside. And it's the thing this pitch has to answer before anyone cares about a token list.

**Why the perception formed — the real origin story.** Mangrove didn't start as a component library. It started as an argument the team had to have and win before anything else could happen: what *is* a card? What *is* a hero? What is the footer, the utility bar above the header, the thing that structures a news article? Those sound like small questions. They weren't. Getting UNDRR's different sites and stakeholders to agree on a shared vocabulary for these atomic content patterns — before a single one of them could be safely reused across platforms — took a long time, and it produced nothing anyone outside the team could see. Once that vocabulary existed, scaling the *agreement* into something every platform could actually run on required a lot of re-architecture: the `data-mg-*` attribute convention, the `createHydrator` runtime, and a `fromElement(el) → props` function per component that turns server-rendered Drupal markup into typed React props deterministically, so a "card" means the same thing whether Drupal or React is rendering it. That's the technical instantiation of the original agreement — not a separate initiative, the *continuation* of it. Call it the content contract: not a term anyone at UNDRR used at the time, and it won't show up in a commit message, but it's the throughline connecting a vocabulary fight in a meeting room to runtime code that now enforces the answer. It's unglamorous, invisible work; it doesn't produce a screenshot. A stakeholder scanning release notes for two years mostly saw dependency bumps, lint tooling, and "component quality" sweeps (`react-doctor`, described below) — work that reads as maintenance, not progress, even when it's the opposite.

**Why 2.0 is the payoff.** That contract is now in place: 8+ components moved onto `createHydrator` with dedicated `fromElement` extraction functions and their own test suites (`docs/HYDRATION-AUTHORING.md`, `docs/HYDRATION.md`), the AI manifest that documents every component's contract for both humans and coding agents has gone through multiple structural passes (60+ manifest-related commits across the project's history), and `react-doctor` — a house-built component-quality linter — moved the codebase's health score from 57 to 68 in a single sweep before 2.0 work even started (v1.7.0, `docs/AI-CODING-AGENTS.md`). None of that shipped a new visual. All of it is why 2.0 could spend its entire budget on things people can *see*: a token system, a contrast methodology, new components, fixed accessibility bugs, and coordinated interaction polish — without also refighting the original "what is a card" argument on every one of UNDRR's ~11 consuming Drupal themes. The foundation being finished is not incidental to 2.0's visible progress. It's the precondition for it.

**And the arc doesn't stop there — it's moving up a level.** The same agreement-then-architecture arc that once had to happen at the component level (what's a card, a hero, a footer) is now happening one level up, at the page-template level: what *is* a landing page? What *is* a content hub section? What *is* a news article, structurally, not just as a stack of cards? These have always existed at UNDRR, but loosely — designed and implemented ad hoc, per product, never standardized as a template any site could just adopt. The new Patterns library (Pillar 3) is the first concrete output of that next round of agreement: a second content contract, this time at the template layer. That's why this pitch should frame 2.0 not as "the polish release" but as "the release where the same hard-won discipline that made components trustworthy starts making whole pages trustworthy."

That's also what makes DELTA the right next conversation, not a random one. A template-level contract is exactly the kind of thing a sub-brand can adopt without losing what makes it a sub-brand: the *thread* is the agreed structure — what sections a content hub has, how a landing page composes, what a news article's skeleton looks like. The *variable* is DELTA's own brand expression inside that structure — its palette, its imagery, its voice. Mangrove was never going to be able to have that conversation with DELTA before the Patterns library existed, because there was nothing concrete to hand over that wasn't also an implicit demand to look like everyone else. Now there is.

**The honest caveat.** This causal story is true but shouldn't be oversold: nobody ran this as a single named initiative. The work emerged from years of hydration, manifest, and code-quality work, done for overlapping but separately-motivated reasons. The pitch is accurate to say 2.0's visible work was made possible *by* that foundation, not that the foundation was built *as a five-year plan* to enable 2.0. Don't let this become "we had a master plan" when the truer story — years of hard-won agreement, first on atoms, now on templates, converging into visible momentum — is more credible anyway.

**Kept deliberately, despite pushback (see §6).** The UX reader called this framing engineering rehearsing to itself, and that criticism is fair as a warning about Section 1's *length*, not its content — a stakeholder pitch shouldn't spend three paragraphs justifying years of invisible work before getting to what's new. It hasn't been cut because the causal claim underneath it is true and load-bearing for the rest of the document: 2.0's visible pillars genuinely depend on this foundation, and saying so once, plainly, earns the right to spend the rest of the pitch on what people can actually see. The honest fix is trimming this section for a shorter version aimed at that audience, not removing the argument.

## 2. The pitch, in four pillars

Verified against `docs/RELEASE-2.0.md`, `CHANGELOG.md`, and `git log v1.8.2..main` (35 squashed PRs, 476 files changed, +36,000/-13,207 lines) in the `unisdr/undrr-mangrove` repo, spanning alpha.1 through beta.1.

### Pillar 1 — Maturity & architecture: the system stopped being hand-maintained

This is the least visible pillar and the one worth leading with for skeptical engineers, because it's the direct rebuttal to "stale."

- **Design tokens moved from hand-edited SCSS to YAML sources** (`tokens/*.yaml`), compiled by `scripts/build-tokens.cjs`, with build failure on unknown/circular references, duplicate output names, or malformed token shapes. `tokens/output-baseline.json` is a committed SHA-256 manifest of generated CSS per theme, so an unintended change to compiled output now fails `yarn test` instead of shipping silently (PR #1087).
- **Theming moved to runtime CSS custom properties** (`--mg-*`) with `.mg-theme-{brand}` selector classes, replacing SCSS-compile-time `!default` variable overrides (PR #1061). One compiled bundle (`style-all.css`) can now serve multiple brands on one page, or switch brand at runtime with no rebuild — previously impossible. **Honest caveat:** "swap a theme class" is a one-line change for anyone consuming the compiled CSS, but not for a theme that imports Mangrove's Sass partials directly — the breaking-changes list in `docs/RELEASE-2.0.md` includes several removed SCSS variable files and aliases, so a Sass-level consumer has real migration work here, not a free upgrade.
- **`npm pack` at the repo root now produces a working tarball** — a real, previously-broken packaging bug (missing `.npmignore` meant `.gitignore` silently excluded compiled SCSS partials from third-party packs) fixed as part of alpha.3.
- **DELTA's actual brand palette was recovered from source.** DELTA's Mangrove theme was built on a navy (`#132e48`) that appears *zero times* in DELTA's own codebase or design file; the real brand blue (`#004f91`, "UNDRR Blue — Corporate blue" in DELTA's own Figma) and a lost secondary teal (`#007b7a`) were restored from DELTA's production repo and DLDTS design file. This is a genuine bug fix, not a redesign — 26 of DELTA's 57 tokens change value or shape as a result.
- **`react-doctor`**, a house-built component-quality linter, is now a standing project convention (`docs/AI-CODING-AGENTS.md`), enforcing rules like no inline render helpers, lazy `useState` init, effect-cleanup discipline, and per-call-site `dangerouslySetInnerHTML` review.
- **Toolchain currency**: Babel 8, React 19.3, TypeScript 6, ESLint 10, Node 22 minimum, Storybook 10.5 — this is not a library running on years-old dependencies.

**Talking point:** "Stale" was true of the surface. It was never true of the plumbing — but nobody could see the plumbing. 2.0 is the release where the plumbing becomes visible as reliability: a broken token silently shipping to five brands is now a build failure, not a support ticket three weeks later.

### Pillar 2 — Accessibility: real defects fixed, not a compliance pass

This is the strongest, most concrete pillar. These are not vague "improved accessibility" claims — they are named, reproducible bugs with before/after states.

- **A checked radio button had no focus indicator at all** — WCAG 2.4.7 (Focus Visible), level A. Two same-specificity `box-shadow` rules were silently overwriting each other; nothing errored, nothing was visible. Fixed by declaring both shadows together at correct specificity (alpha.3).
- **Buttons, chips, and form inputs had no focus indicator at all in forced-colours mode** (Windows High Contrast) — the entire two-band focus treatment was built out of `box-shadow`, which forced-colours mode strips, with `outline: 0` left behind. Fixed by splitting the focus indicator across `outline` (survives forced colours) and `box-shadow` (visual polish), so forced-colours mode degrades to single-band instead of nothing (alpha.3).
- **The keyboard focus ring is no longer visually identical to the selection/interactive colour** — previously `--mg-color-focus-ring` aliased directly to the brand interactive colour, so a focused *and* selected row said the same thing twice with no distinguishing signal. It now resolves to a dedicated, WCAG-graded gold primitive shared across all five themes, following the GOV.UK/NHS/USWDS precedent of a deliberately non-brand focus colour.
- **22 focus-indicator rules across 9 stylesheets** were moved off ad-hoc, non-token colours (hardcoded white, local Sass variables, the UA's own default) onto the shared token. One of those was a literally invisible ring: `MegaMenu`'s section-list link drew a white ring on a white panel (1.28:1 contrast) — now 4.36:1.
- **Desktop axe failures and duplicate element IDs fixed in MegaMenu and PageHeader** (PR #1120) — duplicate IDs break assistive-technology label association; this is the kind of defect that doesn't show up in a design review but does show up in a screen reader.
- **A new `SkipLink` bypass-block component**, wired into the Patterns library, gives keyboard/screen-reader users a way to jump past repeated navigation — a foundational WCAG 2.4.1 (Bypass Blocks) requirement that Mangrove did not previously ship as a reusable component (PR #1119).
- **Every foreground/background token pair is now graded on two independent contrast measures** (WCAG 2 relative luminance and an Oklab-based perceptual measure), with 90 component pairs covered — including, for the first time, the focus ring itself (11 pairs) and the data-visualization palette (18 pairs), neither of which had ever been graded before alpha.3. The build fails if a pair passes perceptually while failing WCAG 2.
- **Arabic-language correctness fixes**: an English quotation or widget embedded inside an Arabic page no longer inherits Arabic typography (a `:lang(ar)` selector bug that had been silently firing on every descendant regardless of that descendant's actual language, fixed across 21 stylesheets); Arabic headings and body text now use a deliberately chosen pairing (Noto Kufi Arabic / Noto Sans Arabic) replacing a font whose EULA forbade the redistribution Mangrove was actually doing.

**Talking point:** These are not "we ran axe and fixed some warnings." A checked radio with zero focus indicator and forced-colours mode losing all focus indicators entirely are real defects that would fail an accessibility audit today, in 1.8.2, right now. 2.0 fixes them with regression-tested, token-backed rules — not one-off CSS patches.

**Honest caveat, worth stating plainly rather than letting a reader assume more than is true:** every accessibility claim above is *measured* — contrast math, automated axe checks, emulated forced-colours mode — not *observed* with a real person using assistive technology. `docs/RELEASE-2.0.md` says this itself: "Forced-colours behaviour has not been verified in real Windows High Contrast, only in emulation... that is reasoned, not observed." That's a meaningfully different claim from "we tested this with real users," and the pitch shouldn't blur the two. The gap between measured and observed accessibility work is real, and closing it — one recorded session with an actual screen-reader or Windows-High-Contrast user completing a real task, on MegaMenu or a form flow — would do more to prove this pillar than any additional contrast number.

### Pillar 3 — New components & patterns: real gaps closed, not padding

Verified by diffing `stories/` between v1.8.2 and main — these did not exist before 2.0:

- **`StatusLabel`** (`.mg-status-label`) — draft/published/waiting-validation/waiting-information states, a real content-workflow gap.
- **`EmptyState`** (`.mg-empty-state`) — compact/panel/start variants, for the "no results" state every search or listing page eventually needs and previously had to be hand-rolled per site.
- **`FormAction`** — joins a field and its primary action (search, subscribe, select-and-continue) with accessible help/error wiring, RTL support, and optional mobile stacking. This directly replaces boilerplate every consuming theme was writing itself.
- **`UserFeedback`** — the standard "was this page helpful" pre-footer prompt, shipped as both a package-root React export and a direct hydratable bundle, in seven Storybook toolbar locales, while deliberately keeping response storage/analytics/consent product-owned (i.e., it doesn't overreach into decisions that belong to each site).
- **`SkipLink`** — bypass-block component (see Pillar 2).
- **A `Patterns` library**, new in this release cycle: **Content Hub**, **Landing Pages**, and **Article Story** reference patterns (PRs #1113, #1125) — full-page compositions, not just atoms. This is the most strategically important item in this pillar, not just the largest: it's the content contract (§1) moved up to the template level, and it's what makes a real DELTA adoption conversation possible (§1 explains why).
- **Tabs redesigned**: centres a compact group when it fits, scrolls horizontally when it overflows, retains panel state through responsive breakpoints, supports optional mobile stacking (`stackOnMobile` / `data-mg-js-tabs-stack-on-mobile`) — enhances existing HTML with no markup migration required at the HTML level (PR #1097; see the Sass-import caveat in Pillar 1 for anyone consuming Mangrove's SCSS partials directly rather than the compiled CSS).
- **MegaMenu rebuilt for mobile**: one navigation level at a time with a proper Back control, translucency/blur effects with opaque motion-free fallbacks for reduced-motion users, focus and scroll restoration on return — backed by 36 test cases across 7 dedicated test files (`.test`, `.a11y.test`, `.overflow.test`, `.progressive.test`, `.redteam.test`, `.exit-redteam.test`, `.fromElement.test`) — a larger investment in correctness than most components in the library have historically had (PR #1100).
- **Cards**: unlinked cards now render as plain text instead of fake-looking links (a real UX-honesty fix, PR #1116); collection spacing and locale fixes (#1110).
- **CTA**: soft-tone variant and composed-content support (#1111).
- **A v2 UI refresh** across tags, sharing, navigation, header and tables (#1106) — this is the closest thing to a "just looks nicer" line item, and it's real, but it's also the item with the least engineering depth in this pillar.

**Honest note:** the Pagination component, deprecated in 1.x, is fully removed in 2.0 (breaking change #6) — consumers still on it must migrate to `Pager` before adopting the prerelease. This is a real migration cost, not zero-friction.

### Pillar 4 — Polish that's actually measured, not vibes

The instinct is to call visual changes "polish" and leave it there. What's notable about 2.0's polish is that most of it is backed by a stated methodology rather than a designer's eyeball:

- **Neutral colour ramp gets a deliberate cool tint** (steps 25–400 only; pure white and near-black are untouched) — motivated by an explicit competitive audit against GOV.UK, USWDS, NHS, EU ECL, OCHA, Primer, Carbon, and Atlassian, which found Mangrove's *pure achromatic greys* were the single largest reason the system read as "generic admin kit." Every graded contrast pair that moves, moves in the direction of *more* contrast, not less — this was checked, not assumed.
- **Two-band focus ring** is now a documented, reusable mixin (`mg-focus-ring` / `mg-focus-ring-inset`) instead of one-off implementations per component, following the same play public-sector systems (GOV.UK, NHS) use so a loud, deliberately non-brand colour has a clean edge against any background.
- **A colour-contrast methodology document** (`docs/COLOUR-CONTRAST-METHODOLOGY.md`) explains why the team evaluated and *rejected* APCA (licensing) in favour of an Oklab-based measure calibrated to WCAG 2's familiar thresholds — a case study in the team publishing its reasoning, not just its output.
- **Sendai Framework colour semantics**: the old `--sendai-red/orange/purple/turquoise` tokens (named by colour, meaning nothing) are deprecated in favour of `--mg-sendai-target-a` through `-g`, which actually map to the seven Sendai targets — a genuine correctness improvement for any dataviz work referencing the framework this whole platform exists to support.
- **The version banner is now embedded in every compiled CSS bundle**, synced from `package.json` — a small thing, but it means "what version of Mangrove is actually deployed on this site" stops being a guessing game.

## 3. Named honestly: what Mangrove still doesn't have

A pitch that only lists what's done reads as defensive to skeptical engineers, who will find the gap themselves anyway. Better to name it first.

Mangrove is now a solid **content and layout baseline**: an agreed content contract at the component level, and — new in 2.0 — the first agreed content contract at the template level via the Patterns library. What it does not have is an **interaction library**: no popover/overlay primitive, no data table or CRUD table pattern, no combobox/complex-select, none of the "how do we build a real application-grade interaction" pieces that a component system eventually needs once consumers start asking for more than content display. This isn't a 2.0 gap specifically — it's never existed in Mangrove — but 2.0 is the first release where the foundation is solid enough that closing it becomes the obvious next move rather than a distraction from more basic problems.

This is worth saying out loud in the pitch, not hiding: it's a concrete, credible answer to "what's next," and it reframes 2.0 from "the release that finally caught up" to "the release that cleared the runway for the next real gap." The live open question — flagged here so it isn't lost — is whether to build these interaction primitives in-house the way Mangrove has built everything else, or adopt a headless interaction-primitives library (React Aria was raised as a candidate) as the foundation underneath Mangrove's own styling and content contracts, the way many public-sector and enterprise design systems now do rather than hand-rolling accessible popover/listbox/table behaviour from scratch. Either path is realistically a 2.1-and-beyond scope, not something to promise as part of this pitch — but naming it signals the team has a plan past 2.0, which matters to an audience deciding whether Mangrove is worth re-investing in.

**Say it plainly for the audience it matters most to.** For a team already productive with Tailwind and MUI — DELTA, for instance — this gap isn't a footnote, it's the actual question. Cards, heroes, and page templates graded on Oklab contrast are not what that team spends most of its time building; forms, tables, and modals are, and that's precisely the part Mangrove doesn't yet have an answer for. (No measured split exists for how much of a typical DELTA sprint that actually is — this is the persona's own framing, not a counted figure, and it deserves an honest number before it's repeated as one.) Pretending otherwise would cost more credibility than admitting it does. The honest pitch to that specific audience is narrower than "adopt Mangrove for everything": it's "adopt Mangrove for content and layout, where it's now genuinely strong, and keep your own tools for interaction until 2.1 closes that gap" — not a full replacement, a partial one, today.

**Open item, not yet answered: real bundle-size numbers.** A skeptical, tooling-fluent developer's sharpest practical question — "what does this cost me, per component, tree-shaken, next to what I'm already paying for MUI or a Tailwind-based equivalent?" — isn't answered anywhere in this pitch or in `docs/RELEASE-2.0.md`. That's a real gap in the evidence, not just in the narrative, and it should be measured and added before this pitch goes in front of engineers who'll ask it immediately.

## 4. How we talk about this

Framing and soundbites for Slack, team meetings, or an eventual announcement — aimed at people who've mentally filed Mangrove under "slow and stale" and need a reason to update that model, not a sales pitch.

- **Lead with the confession, not the feature list.** "Before Mangrove could be a component library, we had to agree, across every product and stakeholder, on what a card is. What a hero is. What the footer and the utility bar are. That argument took a long time and produced nothing you could screenshot. Then we had to rebuild the plumbing so that agreement actually held across every platform, not just on a whiteboard. That's done. 2.0 is the first release where we get to spend the whole budget on things you can actually see."
- **The next act is already visible: templates, not just components.** "We just did the same thing one level up — 'what is a landing page,' 'what is a content hub' — and shipped it as an actual Patterns library instead of another one-off per product. That's also why the DELTA adoption conversation is happening now and not two years ago: there's finally something concrete to adopt."
- **Don't say "redesign." Say "measured."** Nothing in 2.0 is "we made it prettier." Every visible change — the neutral ramp, the focus ring, the DELTA palette fix — has a stated reason and, where relevant, a before/after contrast number. Skeptical engineers respond to "here's the audit that drove this," not "here's what we felt like doing."
- **Lead accessibility with the radio button bug.** "A checked radio button currently has no visible focus indicator in production Mangrove. That's not a style note, that's a WCAG failure that ships today." One concrete, embarrassing, fixed bug does more work than "improved a11y across the board."
- **Be upfront about cost.** Pagination is gone. The 10px legacy root is gone. Six font-family SCSS variables are gone with no compatibility shim. Frame this as "we cut things because we're not carrying three parallel systems forever," not as a hidden landmine — engineers trust a team more when the breaking changes are named plainly and up front (`docs/RELEASE-2.0.md` already does this well; the pitch should point people there rather than re-litigate it).
- **Use real numbers, not round ones.** "36 test cases across 7 files for MegaMenu alone," "22 focus rules across 9 stylesheets," "26 of DELTA's 57 tokens corrected" land harder than "extensively tested" or "major accessibility improvements" — and they're independently checkable, which matters for an audience primed to be skeptical.
- **Resist the urge to claim the content-contract work was master-planned.** It reads better, and is more accurate, to say it emerged from several years of hydration/manifest/quality work done for their own reasons, and 2.0 is where the payoff became visible — not "we had a five-year roadmap."
- **Name the interaction-library gap yourself, before someone else does.** "We know Mangrove still doesn't have a real answer for popovers, data tables, or complex widget interactions. That's the next real gap, and 2.0 is what makes it possible to go close it properly instead of bolting something on." Naming your own gap reads as confidence, not weakness, to an engineering audience — and it sets up whether Mangrove builds this itself or adopts a headless primitives library (React Aria and similar were raised) as a live, still-open question rather than a promise.
- **Name the cost of staying off it, not just the benefit of joining.** This pitch has mostly argued the upside. The sharper argument for people funding decisions, not just making them, is the accumulating cost of the alternative: "every site that stays off Mangrove drifts a little further from a coherent UN identity every day it doesn't adopt it — that's not a one-time gap, it's compounding." A carrot-only pitch is weaker than one that also names what waiting actually costs.

## 5. The demo experience

§4's soundbites are for people already in a room or a Slack thread. This is the format for people who aren't — something that argues for itself without anyone standing next to it explaining the context. It's a concrete plan, not yet built; everything below is a design brief, not a claim about what exists.

**The opening beat: one component, live, reskinning across every UNDRR brand — no reload.** Before the page gets into any 1.8.2-vs-2.0 comparison, open with a single component (MegaMenu is the obvious choice — it's the most visually rich) sitting on screen with a brand toggle: UNDRR blue, PreventionWeb, IRP, MCR2030, DELTA's corrected blue-and-teal, flipping live via the `.mg-theme-{brand}` class swap that 2.0's runtime CSS-custom-property theming actually makes possible (Pillar 1). This is the single highest-leverage moment in the whole demo: a version-over-version slider proves Mangrove *got better*; a live brand-toggle proves Mangrove *is the meeting point this pitch opens by claiming it is* — the same component, instantly re-identifiable as five different organizations, produces a "wait, that's one system?" reaction that a component-level before/after can't produce on its own. It should run first, before the granular comparisons below, so the audience has already felt the "one system, many brands" claim before reading a single word arguing for it.

**Then the core mechanic: live 1.8.2 vs. live 2.0, side by side, scroll-triggered.** A single scrolling page, each section pinned to one specific, previously-documented difference — the MegaMenu underline, the card height-fill behaviour, the focus ring on a checked radio, the DELTA palette fix. At each section, the page splits down the middle with a left-right panning comparison slider: drag or scroll to reveal old on the left, new on the right. The point of doing this *live*, not as screenshots, is that it's honest in a way a curated before/after image never is — a skeptical engineer can open devtools on either pane and see it's real. Two options for how "live" gets implemented, worth deciding early since they have different honesty/effort tradeoffs:

- **Both panes genuinely live**, each an iframe running its own real build — one pinned to the 1.8.2 npm/CDN release, one running current `main`. This is the strongest version of the pitch's own "measured, not vibes" instinct: nothing is staged, the comparison is exactly as real as the library itself.
- **The 2.0 pane live, the 1.8.2 pane a captured reference** (screenshot or short recording) if pinning a working 1.8.2 build in an iframe turns out to be more infrastructure than the payoff is worth. Worth trying the fully-live version first and only falling back to this if it's genuinely not practical — a captured reference undercuts the exact "here's proof, not a claim" argument the pitch has been making throughout.

**The parent page itself runs on Mangrove 2.0** — not a mockup of what Mangrove 2.0 *could* build, the actual thing, dogfooded. That's a claim the pitch can only make credibly if it's literally true.

**Deliberately not a pure Mangrove showcase — the "not a lock-in system" argument.** Alongside the direct component comparisons, the demo should carry a few moments of genuine visual extension beyond what Mangrove ships out of the box — the person planning this has Three.js visualizations in mind as one option. The point isn't spectacle; it's proof that adopting Mangrove doesn't mean surrendering a site's ability to do something distinctive. That's a real, separate objection skeptical engineers raise about design systems generally ("if we adopt this, are we stuck with it") and this demo can answer it in the same breath as the polish argument, without needing its own section.

**Concrete comparison candidates**, pulled directly from work already shipped and documented in this pitch and in `docs/RELEASE-2.0.md` — chosen for being visually legible in a side-by-side slider, not just true in a changelog:

- MegaMenu: the instant-flip underline (1.8.2) vs. the grow-in/fade-out transition (2.0).
- Cards: natural per-card height in separate columns (1.8.2) vs. `block-size: 100%` fill (2.0).
- Focus states: the invisible/zero-indicator radio button and forced-colours failure (1.8.2) vs. the token-backed two-band focus ring (2.0).
- DELTA theme: the wrong navy that never appeared in DELTA's own brand source (1.8.2) vs. the recovered brand blue and teal (2.0).
- Neutral colour ramp: the flat achromatic grey (1.8.2) vs. the cool-tinted, higher-contrast ramp (2.0).

**Proposed build approach**: an Astro site, reusing the existing microsite tooling's React-bootstrapping layer rather than building that integration from scratch — Astro's island-hydration model is a natural fit for a page that's mostly static scroll-driven prose with a handful of live, independently-interactive component islands (the comparison sliders, any Three.js pieces), instead of hydrating the whole page as one React app.

## 6. Persona discovery: what three readers actually said

Before this draft went further, it was read in character by three people this pitch needs to convince, each played by a specialized agent grounded in the actual codebase and release notes rather than just the pitch's own claims: a DELTA developer already comfortable with Tailwind/MUI, a UX/design-side reader, and someone from communications who cares about consistency and branding across UNDRR's properties. This section is a record of what they actually said, so the reasoning behind the edits above (and the open items still unaddressed) is traceable rather than asserted.

**The DELTA developer** was moved by the checkable specifics — the DELTA palette fix especially, because it's independently verifiable against DELTA's own Figma — but was blunt that this document argues "Mangrove isn't stale," not "you should replace what you're already using." §3's own admission that Mangrove has no popover, table, or combobox is, for this reader, the entire ballgame: most of their daily work is forms, tables, and modals, which Mangrove doesn't yet cover. They also flagged that "no markup migration required" claims deserve scrutiny given the real Sass-level breaking changes, and that nothing in this pitch or `docs/RELEASE-2.0.md` gives a real bundle-size number next to what they're already paying for MUI or Tailwind. Both are now reflected above (§3, Pillar 1).

**The UX reader** responded to the accessibility bugs and the DELTA story specifically because they're framed as user-facing failures with a before/after, not "we improved a11y" — but pushed back hard on §1's "content contract" framing as engineering rehearsing to itself, and made the sharpest single point in this round: every accessibility claim in the document is *measured* (contrast math, emulated forced-colours), not *observed* with a real person using assistive technology — a distinction `docs/RELEASE-2.0.md` itself admits ("reasoned, not observed"). That's now called out explicitly in Pillar 2. They also flagged that nothing here addresses design-to-code fidelity or a single real usability session — that gap is not yet closed, only named (see §7).

**The comms reader** was, as expected, the easiest sell — the "meeting point" framing and the three-audiences paragraph landed immediately, because they're the person who fields "why doesn't PreventionWeb's header match undrr.org's" on a regular basis. Their contribution was pointing out that the pitch's best asset, the sub-brand theming architecture, was buried as a bug-fix anecdote (the DELTA palette story) rather than staged as a demonstration in its own right — and that the demo plan in §5 didn't yet include a moment where a visitor watches one component reskin live across every UNDRR brand. Both are now reflected above (§5's new opening beat). They also named a gap this pitch hadn't addressed at all: the cost of *not* adopting Mangrove, which is now a soundbite in §4.

**What's still open, unresolved by this round:** a real per-component bundle-size comparison; one recorded usability session with a real assistive-technology user; and a design-to-code/Figma-fidelity story, which nobody involved in this draft so far has the standing to write credibly — it needs an actual design-ops or Figma-library owner, not another round of persona simulation.

## 7. What this document is NOT, and next steps

**This is a strategy/pitch draft**, written to sharpen the argument and assemble verified evidence — not a document meant to ship as-is. It has not been reviewed by the rest of the team, and some claims (particularly the causal "content contracts enabled 2.0" framing) are a specific interpretive stance, not a fact recorded anywhere in the repo's own history. Treat the evidence in Pillars 1–4 as solid; treat the framing and soundbites as a first draft to be argued with.

**Recommended next steps, in order:**

1. ~~Sanity-check the framing with one or two engineers who hold the skeptical view.~~ **Done, in simulated form (§6).** A real conversation with an actual skeptical engineer — not just a persona agent grounded in the codebase — is still worth having before this goes further; the simulation is a strong first pass, not a substitute.
2. **Close the three items §6 flagged as still open**: a real per-component bundle-size comparison against MUI/Tailwind equivalents, one recorded usability session with a real assistive-technology user, and a design-to-code/Figma-fidelity story from someone who actually owns that relationship.
3. **Don't build this inside Mangrove-as-Storybook.** Storybook is optimized for "here is a component and its props," not for a narrative argument with prose sections, and burying a pitch document inside the same tool that skeptics already associate with "the thing that feels stale" undercuts the message. It also creates an awkward audience mismatch: Storybook's primary audience is people already using Mangrove technically, not the stakeholders this pitch needs to reach.
4. **A standalone internal blog post or all-hands doc is the better home** for a refined version of this prose — something you can link directly in Slack, distinct from technical docs, that doesn't require anyone to already be in a Storybook mindset to read it.
5. **Build the live comparison demo described in §5**, opening beat and all. It's a stronger, more honest version of "before/after screenshots" — real 1.8.2 vs. real 2.0, scroll-driven, provable by anyone who opens devtools, plus the live brand-toggle moment — and it does more work than any amount of additional prose in this document.
6. **Keep `docs/RELEASE-2.0.md` as the technical source of truth** and let this pitch link to it for people who want the full detail — don't duplicate the breaking-changes list here; it's already well-maintained there.
